What are my rights as a passenger for delayed flights?

As a passenger, your rights for delayed flights are governed by U.S. Department of Transportation regulations. If your flight is delayed for more than three hours, you may be entitled to compensation, including refunds or travel vouchers, depending on the circumstances of the delay. It's important to document your experience and communicate with the airline to understand the compensation options available to you.

How do I file a compensation claim with the airline?

To file a compensation claim with the airline, start by gathering all relevant documentation, such as your ticket, boarding pass, and any communication regarding the delay. Visit the airline's official website and look for their customer service or claims section, where you can find specific instructions for submitting your claim. Be clear and concise in your claim submission, detailing the nature of the delay and any inconvenience caused.

What should I do if my claim is denied?

If your claim is denied, you have several options to pursue further. First, review the airline's response to understand their reasoning for the denial. You can then consider escalating the issue by contacting a consumer advocacy group or filing a complaint with the U.S. Department of Transportation. Additionally, you may want to seek legal advice if you believe your rights have been violated.
