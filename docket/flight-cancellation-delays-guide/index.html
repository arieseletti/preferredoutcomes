What happened and who qualifies for compensation during flight disruptions?

Recent reports indicate a significant increase in flight cancellations and delays, affecting travelers across the United States. Those holding confirmed tickets for flights that are canceled or delayed beyond a certain time threshold may be eligible for compensation under federal regulations. Affected travelers should know that they have rights to refunds, alternative travel arrangements, and sometimes additional compensation for inconveniences. Many airlines, however, do not readily inform passengers of these rights, leading to confusion and frustration.

Why do standard company responses or class actions often shortchange victims of flight disruptions?

Airlines frequently respond to claims with boilerplate denials or offer minimal compensation, claiming that circumstances were beyond their control. Additionally, class action lawsuits can be lengthy and complex, often resulting in victims receiving negligible compensation after legal fees—sometimes as high as 40%. This leaves many consumers feeling cheated, as they miss out on their rightful compensation due to drawn-out legal processes. Moreover, airlines may distract travelers with offers of free credit monitoring instead of addressing the root of the issue.
